**Research Focus**: Modeling, analysis, and optimization of key features in next-generation IEEE 802.11 (Wi-Fi) systems, with a focus on cross-layer interactions between the physical and MAC layers
My research draws on stochastic modeling and optimization techniques, including Markov models, renewal theory, fixed-point analysis, Markov decision processes (MDPs), and cross-layer optimization, to analyze and optimize wireless network performance.

**Hybrid access OFDMA in IEEE 802.11ax/Wi-Fi 6:**

*	Designed an optimal policy that dynamically allocates resources for random and scheduled access, addressing the fundamental trade-off between saturation throughput and average access delay

*	Developed a cross-layer analysis that accurately characterizes the saturation throughput in time-varying channels to account for small-scale fading and feedback delays

*Associated publication:*

[J1] S. Arthi, N. B. Mehta, and C. Singh, “Hybrid access MAC protocol in Wi-Fi: Analysis and optimal resource allocation policy design,” IEEE Trans. Mob. Comput., vol. 25, no. 4, pp. 5742-5757, Apr. 2026.

[J2] S. Arthi and N. B. Mehta, “S. Arthi and N. B. Mehta, "Cross-layer throughput analysis of hybrid-access Wi-Fi in time-Varying channels," IEEE Wireless Commun. Lett., vol. 15, pp. 3676-3680, Jun. 2026.

[C1] S. Arthi and N. B. Mehta, “Saturation throughput analysis of hybrid access MAC protocol in IEEE 802.11ax WLANs,” in Proc. IEEE Glob. Commun. Conf (GLOBECOM), Cape Town, South Africa, Dec. 2024.

**Multi-link operation with uplink OFDMA random access in IEEE 802.11be/Wi-Fi 7:**

*	Characterized the throughput and access delay performance for simultaneous (STR) and non-simultaneous (NSTR) multi-link operation modes with and without legacy users

*	Identified the operating regimes in which the shortest/longest back-off rule yielded a higher throughput for NSTR with OFDMA-based random access

*	Determined the optimal contention parameters for the access point that ensured a fair co-existence between multi-link and legacy users
